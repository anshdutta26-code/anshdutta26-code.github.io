Ontario Science Centre Rebranding & IMC

Academic brand strategy case study covering positioning, identity, messaging, experiential marketing, OOH, PR, social media and SEO/SEM for the Ontario Science Centre.

View Full Google Doc ↗All Projects

Project Type

Postgraduate Academic Rebranding & Integrated Marketing Communications Project

Primary Focus

Research, audience strategy, positioning, identity, messaging, experiential concept, social, OOH, PR and SEO/SEM

Project Snapshot

Project output: 35-page rebranding and integrated marketing communications strategy. Audience scope: Families, educators/students, GTA tourists and corporate groups. Commercial status: Academic strategy project; no live attendance/revenue result is claimed.

Project Context

The Ontario Science Centre is a major interactive science institution in Toronto with a broad mandate spanning education, discovery and public engagement. The project began by reviewing the Centre's existing marketing activity—including exhibitions, memberships, digital content, events and partnerships—and asking how the brand could feel more contemporary, coherent and relevant to multiple audiences while still supporting its educational mission.

The Objective

The objective was to build a complete rebranding and IMC direction rather than produce a logo in isolation. The strategy needed to clarify positioning, refresh the visual identity, define the audiences, establish a consistent messaging platform and then show how that brand system could come alive through experiences and communications across advertising, social media, public relations, outdoor media and search.

My Role & Ownership

I worked as part of the postgraduate project team and contributed to the research, strategic thinking, brand direction and integrated communications plan. My work included audience/competitor analysis, positioning and messaging logic, brand identity development, campaign/experience concepts and digital/search recommendations, with the final thinking brought together into a presentation-ready strategy.

What I Worked On / Built

  • Reviewed current branding and marketing activity including temporary exhibitions, awareness campaigns, attendance-driving events/promotions, memberships, digital content and partnerships.
  • Defined four key audience groups: families with children under 18, educators/students, visitors to the Greater Toronto Area and corporate groups.
  • Benchmarked science-centre competitors such as Science North, Science World Vancouver and TELUS World of Science to understand category expectations and differentiation opportunities.
  • Developed positioning around 'Where the Future Takes Shape' and a visual identity direction designed to communicate wonder, discovery and innovation.
  • Built messaging pillars around curiosity, accessibility/inclusivity, education and innovation, supported by an engaging, approachable and enthusiastic tone of voice.
  • Mapped how the identity and messaging would remain consistent while adapting across advertising, social media, PR and other marketing channels.
  • Developed an experiential 'Science Escape Room' concept using themed scientific challenges and RFID-supported progress/hints to combine education, teamwork and entertainment.
  • Created social activation ideas such as teaser trailers, behind-the-scenes content and countdown campaigns.
  • Proposed OOH 'Billboard Constellations' using illuminated star patterns and science facts/event messages to create a more interactive outdoor idea.
  • Proposed a Scientist-in-Residence PR program supported by universities/research institutions, lectures, workshops, Q&As, press releases and media interviews.
  • Built SEO/SEM recommendations including long-tail theme keywords, optimized titles/meta/headings and educational/content-led search strategy.

How I Approached It

The project was structured from strategy outward: understand the institution and its audiences first, define the position and identity second, establish messaging rules third, and only then design campaign executions. This kept the experiential and media ideas connected to a clear brand promise rather than becoming a list of disconnected tactics. The IMC model emphasized one consistent identity with channel-specific adaptation.

Impact & Outcome

  • Produced a 35-page end-to-end rebranding and IMC strategy rather than a single creative concept.
  • Connected brand identity, messaging and channel execution into one integrated framework.
  • Developed ideas across experiential, social, OOH, PR and search, demonstrating both creative and strategic range.
  • Created a clear audience structure that allowed different messages and activations to be designed without losing overall brand consistency.
  • The work served as a postgraduate portfolio project and received positive academic recognition; because it was not a live Ontario Science Centre implementation, I do not claim changes in attendance, revenue or brand metrics.

What Happened

The final work was completed and presented as an academic rebranding/IMC strategy. Its value in my portfolio is the breadth of the thinking: research, positioning, identity, messaging, experiential design and multi-channel activation were treated as one system. The campaign recommendations were strategic proposals rather than live executions, so the outcome is the quality and completeness of the plan rather than a post-launch commercial result.

Key Challenges & Decisions

  • Modernizing the brand without losing its educational credibility and institutional heritage.
  • Serving very different audiences—families, students, tourists and corporate groups—within one coherent position.
  • Making scientific learning feel entertaining and culturally relevant without trivializing the subject matter.
  • Keeping the messaging consistent while adapting it to very different channels and experiences.
  • Separating creative ambition from measurable channel objectives and proposed measurement methods.
