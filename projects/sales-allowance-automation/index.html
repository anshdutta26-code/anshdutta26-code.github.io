Sales Allowance & TADA Automation

Case study of a policy-driven Excel and Google Sheets travel-expense workflow covering local and outstation travel, kilometre logic, tickets, hotel and allowances.

View Full Google Doc ↗All Projects

Project Type

Professional Operations / Excel & Google Sheets Process-Automation Project

Primary Focus

Travel policy mapping, claim structure, mode-based rules, formulas, validations, support documentation and approval controls

Project Snapshot

Primary users: Field sales employees, reporting managers and Accounts/Admin reviewers. Control model: Employee-input claim fields + protected formula totals + office-only approval fields. Documentation: 4-page visual filling manual explaining day-to-day claim rules and support requirements.

Project Context

Field-sales travel claims become difficult when the policy exists in words but employees, managers and Accounts interpret it differently in the daily sheet. Local travel, upcountry travel, bike kilometres, bus/train/flight tickets, hotel bills, meals and other expenses all behave differently. The risk was not only wrong arithmetic; it was inconsistent filing, duplicate or unsupported claims, overwritten formulas and repeated clarification between employees and reviewers.

The Objective

The objective was to turn travel-policy rules into a structured, easy-to-fill system. Employees needed to know exactly which fields they were responsible for, which amounts required bills/support, which totals must never be edited, and which approval fields belonged only to Accounts/Admin. The finished format also needed to create a clearer audit trail for tickets, hotel and meal support.

My Role & Ownership

I mapped the claim process and policy logic, designed the workbook structure, defined the employee-entry versus formula/office-only boundaries, built calculation and validation logic, and created the visual filling manual used to explain the system. A major part of my role was translating operational policy into instructions that a salesperson could follow correctly without having to interpret the policy from scratch every time.

What I Worked On / Built

  • Structured the daily claim fields around date, from/to town or city, travel type, mode, kilometres and the relevant claim columns.
  • Separated Local TADA, Upcountry/Outstation Travel and Ticket Amount as employee-entered claim fields while protecting formula-driven total/page-total cells and office-only approval cells.
  • Defined the Local travel rule: mark KM and claim only Local TADA as per policy; Upcountry/Outstation Travel and Ticket Amount remain blank/zero unless specifically approved.
  • Defined Upcountry by Bike logic: mark KM and calculate the eligible travel claim at KM × 7 in the Upcountry/Outstation Travel field; the bike KM claim is not entered as Ticket Amount.
  • Defined Upcountry by Bus logic: mark KM for the trip, enter the bus fare in Ticket Amount, and do not duplicate the same fare in Upcountry/Outstation Travel.
  • Defined Train/Flight/Taxi support: Ticket Amount is entered in the daily sheet and the last/support sheet captures ticket/PNR/bill details; multiple ticket numbers must be clearly recorded.
  • Defined hotel controls: enter hotel amounts day-wise; if one bill covers multiple nights, provide a date-wise breakup/remarks and attach support.
  • Defined meal-claim controls: claims must follow policy and supporting-bill requirements; alcohol is not reimbursable.
  • Created a support-sheet structure so ticket amount and ticket/PNR/bill information can be checked against the daily claim for the same date.
  • Protected total amount claimed/page totals from employee editing and kept Total Amount Approved for office use only.
  • Created a 4-page visual manual with color-coded instructions showing manual-entry, bill/support, formula/no-edit and office-only fields.

How I Approached It

The design principle was 'make the policy visible in the workflow.' Rather than relying on people to remember every rule, the sheet and manual separate what the employee enters, what requires support, what the spreadsheet calculates, and what only the office can approve. The corrected version also makes an important distinction: Local TADA, Upcountry/Outstation Travel and Ticket Amount are manual claim fields; only totals/page totals and approval fields are protected/automatic. This prevents a common misunderstanding about which cells should calculate themselves.

Impact & Outcome

  • Standardized how field-sales employees record local, upcountry and ticket-based travel claims.
  • Reduced policy ambiguity by documenting mode-specific rules directly against the claim columns employees use.
  • Separated employee claim entry from formula totals and office approval, reducing the risk of formulas or approval cells being overwritten.
  • Created a stronger audit trail by linking ticket amounts to ticket/PNR/bill support and requiring hotel/meal documentation rules.
  • Reduced avoidable back-and-forth between employees and reviewers by giving both sides the same visual rulebook.
  • Converted a policy-heavy process into a repeatable operating format that could be reused month after month.

What Happened

The final system was supported by a dedicated four-page filling manual. The manual did more than explain where to type; it resolved the most important interpretation issue in the format by explicitly stating which claim columns are employee-filled and which cells are formula/office controlled. That made the workbook easier to administer and easier for field users to follow consistently. I do not claim a quantified cost or time reduction because that was not formally measured in the supplied material; the demonstrated value is standardization, control and review-readiness.

Key Challenges & Decisions

  • Translating policy language into spreadsheet logic without creating a workbook that was too complicated for field use.
  • Avoiding double claims—for example, a bus fare should appear in Ticket Amount rather than also being repeated as an Upcountry/Outstation amount.
  • Ensuring employees understand that 'Auto' as a travel mode does not mean the claim is automatically calculated.
  • Handling multi-ticket journeys, ticket numbers/PNRs and matching support amounts to the daily sheet.
  • Preventing copy/paste actions from overwriting totals or office-only approval cells.
  • Capturing hotel bills correctly when a multi-night stay is charged in a single transaction.
